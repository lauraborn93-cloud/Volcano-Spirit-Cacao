The Spirit of Cacao

About Ceremonial Cacao

For more than 4,000 years, cacao has been grown, fermented, ground, and shared by Mayan hands as a gift from the earth.

Today, most of that story has been lost and replaced by monocultures, exploitation, and candy bars that barely contain cacao at all.

Our cacao hasn't forgotten where it came from — and neither will you, once you taste it.

The Hidden Cost of Cheap Chocolate

Chocolate has a supply chain problem.

Most chocolate has very little to do with cacao. What begins as one of nature's most nutrient-dense plants is often transformed into a product filled with sugar, flavorings, emulsifiers, and cheap ingredients designed for mass production.

At the same time, many cacao farmers receive only a small fraction of the final value of the products sold around the world. The people who cultivate, harvest, ferment, and dry the cacao — while keeping the wisdom and tradition alive — are often the ones who benefit the least from its success. The result is a system that values volume over quality, and profit over connection.

From Living Forests, Not Monocultures

Where cacao grows shapes how it feels — in your body, and on the planet.

Our cacao comes from biodiverse agroforestry systems, while much industrial chocolate is sourced from large-scale monocultures that prioritize yield over ecosystem health.

Our Agroforestry Cacao Forests

Our cacao trees grow in lush forests alongside fruit, timber, and other native plants. Birds, insects, and rich soil life thrive here, and farmers work in close relationship with the land, in conditions that are not only sustainable, but regenerative.

No chemicals or pesticides are used here, and cacao is harvested by hand, then dried, fermented, and roasted by the people who have worked with it for generations.

vs.

Monoculture Plantations

Much conventional chocolate is sourced from vast monoculture plantations, where a single crop stretches for acres with little shade or habitat for other species.

These systems often rely on heavy agrochemical use, which can deplete soil health and pollute local ecosystems. Workers in these supply chains often face harsh, exploitative conditions, with little transparency and few protections.

Why People Love Ceremonial Cacao

Emotional and physical — never medical.

Emotional & Spiritual

We are not allowed to make any health claims due to regulations in the EU.

We believe cacao is a wonderful ally for ceremony and meditation practices, as well as small daily rituals. It is often used to connect more with our hearts, allowing for a deeper sense of presence and emotional connection.

Physical

Unlike heavily processed chocolate, ceremonial cacao preserves the whole cacao bean, with its naturally occurring cacao butter and plant compounds intact. That's why many people choose ceremonial cacao as an alternative to conventional hot chocolate or coffee — a way to slow down and reconnect.
